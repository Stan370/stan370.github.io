After creating the TonyChat repository in January this year, I started teaching myself TypeScript, Node.js, and other mainstream full-stack technologies while designing the most practical technical architecture for the project. I had to think through what the core product features should be, what users actually need, and how to communicate with the open-source community. Having complete autonomy over my work is a double-edged sword: on one hand, I can arrange my schedule freely as long as the work gets done; on the other hand, my dislike of rigid planning and tendency to improvise leave me easily distracted by entertainment, so my productivity isn’t as high as it is under external structure.

Here’s my personal reflection: the world is harsh, and to stand out or achieve success in the conventional sense, you need a clear grasp of how it actually operates. Beneath the veneer of morality and law, reality can often resemble a jungle where people fiercely compete for leverage and influence through negotiation, deception, and exchanges of interest until they build an unshakable foothold within their sphere. Yet when your capabilities cannot yet support your ambitions, you need to stay humble, act thoughtfully, and keep putting in the work.

Seeking power isn’t inherently bad—ruthlessness is. The ruthless pursuit of power violates a core principle of ethics: Kant’s Categorical Imperative. As Kant put it, "Act only on that maxim whereby you can, at the same time, will that it should become a universal law." In other words, don’t do it yourself if you wouldn’t want everyone else to follow your example. Benighted self-interest is toxic; enlightened self-interest is empowering.

Human traits are multifaceted, carrying both strengths and shadows. Toughness or detachment is often frowned upon, yet when channeled constructively, it can become a powerful asset: firm resolve, ambition, and unwavering focus on a goal can carry us forward. Still, balance is essential—our ambition must never eclipse empathy and compassion for others.

So let’s remember that just as light needs darkness to be appreciated, the constructive edge of resolve can teach us vital lessons in resilience, grit, and aiming high. By recognizing and accepting this duality in human traits, we can harness their strength to build a meaningful and fulfilling life. It is by confronting hardship and dark stretches that people genuinely grow and learn to value the light; adversity builds resilience, wisdom, and gratitude—and pushes us to tackle the real bottlenecks: experimental design, statistical rigor, team alignment, and long-term architecture.
